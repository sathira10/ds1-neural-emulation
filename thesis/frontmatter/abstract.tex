\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This dissertation investigates whether a compact deep learning model can emulate the nonlinear response of a Boss DS-1 guitar distortion pedal accurately enough for real-time software use, and whether a gated recurrent unit (GRU) or long short-term memory (LSTM) architecture offers the stronger balance of accuracy and computational efficiency. The project follows a practice-based research paradigm in which original artefacts, including a matched dry/wet recording dataset, trained neural models, a custom export pipeline, and a deployed audio plugin, form both the method and the outcome of the study. Original audio recordings are captured from a researcher-owned Boss DS-1 under controlled conditions and used to train GRU-64 and LSTM-64 models with an otherwise identical Conv1D to recurrent to Dense architecture. The models are evaluated using Error-to-Signal Ratio (ESR), parameter count, and inference timing on a held-out test set, while the selected LSTM model is additionally exported and deployed as a real-time plugin for testing in a digital audio workstation. Final findings will determine whether the simpler GRU delivers comparable fidelity to the LSTM at lower computational cost, and whether the deployed model performs reliably within practical real-time constraints. The study contributes a documented end-to-end workflow from hardware capture to plugin deployment and addresses a specific gap in the literature by comparing GRU and LSTM directly on Boss DS-1 emulation under matched conditions.
